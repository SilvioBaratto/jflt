# JFLT Writing Simulation

## Task 1 — Sanctions Framework Renewal: Request for Clarification
*Level 3 · formal_correspondence · 150–250 words*

You are a staff officer (Major, J5 Political-Military Affairs) at a Multinational Corps Headquarters. The joint sanctions framework agreed under the Bremerhaven Accord is due to expire at the end of the calendar year. Several partner nations have signalled support for renewal and potential strengthening of the measures, but your headquarters has not yet received a formal written position from one partner nation.

Write a formal letter to that partner nation's Defence Attaché. Your letter must:

- State the purpose of the letter clearly and identify the relevant framework by name and expiry date.
- Explain the current status of partner consultations and why the partner nation's position is needed.
- Request written clarification on three specific points: support for renewal; proposed modifications, if any; and the designated national focal point for further consultations.
- State a clear response deadline and explain why it is necessary.
- Use a register and conventions appropriate to formal inter-governmental military correspondence.

## Task 2 — Procurement Recommendation: Supply Chain Diversification
*Level 3 · formal_correspondence · 150–250 words*

You are a senior logistics officer in a national defence headquarters. A recent independent assessment has identified a critical dependency on a single overseas supplier for high-precision inertial navigation units used in an armoured reconnaissance vehicle programme. Geopolitical developments in the supplier's region have elevated the risk of supply disruption significantly.

Write a formal email to the Director of Procurement recommending urgent action. Your email must:

- Identify the component, the nature of the dependency, and the geopolitical risk factor.
- Summarise the key finding of the independent assessment, including the potential impact on the vehicle programme.
- Recommend at least three concrete measures to mitigate the risk.
- Request a follow-up meeting and offer to provide the full assessment on request.
- Maintain a formal, professional register appropriate to an internal advisory communication between senior officers.

## Task 3 — Conscription and NATO Readiness: A Strategic Assessment
*Level 3 · essay · 250–500 words*

Several NATO member states are actively debating whether to reintroduce mandatory military service in response to a deteriorating security environment and persistent shortfalls in professional force recruitment. Proponents argue that conscription strengthens deterrence and societal resilience; critics question whether it delivers the technical proficiency modern warfare demands.

Write an analytical essay examining whether NATO member states should reintroduce conscription. Your essay must:

- Present a clear, arguable thesis in the introduction.
- Examine the principal arguments in favour of reintroduction, supported by specific reasoning or examples.
- Present and engage seriously with the main counterarguments or limitations.
- Consider at least one alternative approach to addressing the underlying problem.
- Reach a reasoned conclusion consistent with the argument developed.
- Maintain an analytical, impersonal register appropriate to policy analysis throughout.

## Task 4 — Energy Diversification and Geopolitical Resilience
*Level 3 · essay · 250–500 words*

The disruption of energy supply has emerged as a key instrument of geopolitical coercion. Several states have responded by pursuing diversification of their energy sources and supply routes, presenting this as a central pillar of their national security strategy. However, analysts disagree on whether diversification alone is sufficient to reduce strategic vulnerability.

Write an analytical essay evaluating the extent to which energy market diversification effectively reduces geopolitical vulnerability for energy-importing states. Your essay must:

- State a clear position in the introduction that goes beyond a simple description of the issue.
- Present evidence or reasoning that supports the case for diversification as a geopolitical tool.
- Critically examine the limitations or risks of diversification strategies.
- Distinguish between energy independence and energy security, explaining why the distinction matters.
- Conclude with a substantiated judgement on the overall effectiveness of diversification as a geopolitical strategy.
- Write in an analytical, impersonal register consistent with quality policy journalism or a strategic brief.

## Task 5 — Supply-Chain Briefing to the Minister of Trade

The Minister of Trade has requested a formal briefing note outlining the situation and recommending concrete policy action.

Write a briefing note to the Minister of Trade in which you:

- Identify two specific vulnerabilities that recent disruptions have exposed in the national supply chain
- Propose at least two concrete policy measures to strengthen supply-chain resilience
- Acknowledge the short-term economic costs of your proposed measures and address them briefly
- Maintain a formal, analytical register appropriate to an official policy document

## Task 6 — Governing Artificial Intelligence: The Case for Balanced Regulation
*Level 3 · essay · 250–500 words*

Governments worldwide are under pressure to regulate artificial intelligence. Some argue that strict regulation is essential to prevent algorithmic discrimination, disinformation, and loss of human oversight; others warn that heavy-handed intervention will stifle innovation and drive development to less scrupulous jurisdictions.

Write an essay in which you:

- Present a clear thesis on how AI governance should be approached
- Analyse at least two risks associated with excessive regulation
- Analyse at least two risks associated with insufficient regulation
- Acknowledge the strongest counter-argument to your position and address it
- Conclude with a reasoned policy recommendation

## Task 9 — Aide-Mémoire on Sanctions Circumvention
*Level 4 · formal_correspondence · 250–400 words*

You are the Political Director at your country's Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Reliable intelligence assessments and joint coalition financial-tracking mechanisms indicate that a close ally — a fellow signatory of a multilateral sanctions regime against a third state — has been systematically circumventing the sanctions through third-country commercial intermediaries, materially undermining the regime's effectiveness and creating significant legal and reputational exposure for the coalition.

Draft a confidential aide-mémoire to the Ambassador of the offending ally in which you:
- acknowledge the bilateral strategic partnership while registering the gravity of the concern;
- set out, with precision, the nature of the alleged circumvention and its systemic implications for the coalition;
- invoke the treaty or coalition-framework obligation that the ally's conduct places in jeopardy;
- request a senior-level bilateral consultation within a defined timeframe;
- indicate, without issuing an explicit ultimatum, the potential consequences of inaction.

Write 250–400 words. Use appropriate diplomatic-note conventions (compliments formula, third-person register, closing assurance).

## Task 10 — Nuclear Deterrence in the Age of Emerging Threats
*Level 4 · essay · 350–700 words*

Nuclear deterrence has traditionally rested on the logic of mutual assured destruction — the assumption that the certainty of devastating retaliation would preclude rational first-strike decisions. The proliferation of hypersonic missiles, the weaponisation of cyberspace, and the integration of artificial intelligence into early-warning and command-and-control architectures are now challenging the foundational conditions on which that logic depended.

Write an analytical essay in which you:
- identify the principal ways in which emerging technologies are altering the strategic environment for nuclear deterrence;
- assess whether the mutual assured destruction paradigm retains validity under these conditions;
- acknowledge the strongest argument for doctrinal continuity before refuting or qualifying it;
- advance a considered position on whether, and how, deterrence doctrine should be revised.

Write 350–700 words. State a clear thesis in your introduction and ensure your conclusion directly addresses the question.

## Task 11 — Position Paper on Loss-and-Damage Finance
*Level 4 · formal_correspondence · 250–400 words*

You are the Head of Delegation for your country at an international climate finance summit. Negotiations on a proposed loss-and-damage compensation mechanism have stalled: two major contributing states are conditioning their pledges on governance and accountability criteria that smaller, climate-vulnerable nations regard as discriminatory conditionality. The summit chair has asked senior delegates to submit a formal position paper ahead of the ministerial session.

Draft a formal position paper addressed to the Chair in which you:
- state your country's position on the proposed loss-and-damage mechanism and on the nature of the impasse;
- analyse the governance criteria being insisted upon by the contributing states and explain why they are or are not acceptable in their current form;
- propose a specific compromise framework that addresses contributing states' accountability concerns without imposing disproportionate burdens on recipient states;
- request that the Chair incorporate your proposals into the draft ministerial communiqué.

Write 250–400 words. Use the conventions of a formal position paper (header, subject line, structured paragraphs, sign-off).

## Task 12 — The Multilateral Order Under Great-Power Pressure
*Level 4 · essay · 350–700 words*

The post-1945 international order was constructed around multilateral institutions — the United Nations system, the Bretton Woods bodies, and their successors — premised on universal membership and rules-based cooperation. The resurgence of great-power competition and the rise of parallel institutional architectures (the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation, BRICS+) have prompted intense debate about whether the liberal multilateral order is merely under strain or is irreversibly giving way to a more fragmented, plurilateral world.

Write an analytical essay in which you:
- examine the evidence that the liberal multilateral order is in structural decline;
- assess the strongest counter-evidence that the institutions retain functional relevance;
- advance an overall characterisation of the current trajectory — erosion, eclipse, transformation, or fragmentation — with supporting reasoning;
- consider the implications of your characterisation for the management of genuinely global challenges.

Write 350–700 words. State a clear thesis, develop a structured argument across distinct body paragraphs, and end with a conclusion that directly addresses the question.

## Task 13 — Diplomatic Note: Contesting a Unilateral Digital Services Tax
*Level 4 · formal_correspondence · 250–400 words*

You are the Director of International Economic Policy at your country's Ministry of Finance. The Republic of Veldania, a major trading partner, has enacted a 3% Digital Services Tax (DST) levied on the gross revenues of companies deriving more than €50 million annually from Veldanian digital advertising and online intermediation markets. Several technology companies domiciled in your country are subject to the DST. Your Ministry's legal analysis concludes that the measure is inconsistent with the national treatment clause of the bilateral investment treaty in force between the two countries and conflicts with both parties' commitments under the ongoing OECD/G20 Pillar One negotiations.

Write a formal diplomatic note to the Veldanian Deputy Minister of Finance in which you:

- Formally convey your government's legal objection to the DST, identifying the specific treaty and multilateral framework provisions you consider to be engaged
- Quantify the economic impact on companies domiciled in your jurisdiction using concrete figures
- Propose that both states agree to suspend the domestic measure pending the formal adoption and entry into force of the Pillar One multilateral agreement
- Request an urgent bilateral technical meeting at senior official level within three weeks

Observe the full conventions of an inter-governmental diplomatic note throughout, including the protocolary opening and closing formulae.

## Task 14 — Essay: Democratic Governance of Artificial Intelligence
*Level 4 · essay · 350–700 words*

Assess the following claim: "The regulatory frameworks governing artificial intelligence in civilian and dual-use contexts are structurally incapable of keeping pace with the rate of technological development, creating accountability gaps that democratic societies have not yet found effective mechanisms to close."

Your essay must:

- Advance a nuanced, arguable thesis that goes beyond simply agreeing or disagreeing with the claim as stated
- Identify and evaluate the strongest evidence in support of the structural incapacity argument
- Present and rigorously interrogate the principal counter-arguments, with particular attention to how regulatory design could address the problem
- Treat the specific challenge posed by dual-use AI systems as a distinct and particularly intractable governance problem
- Arrive at a reasoned conclusion that draws the threads of the argument together and responds directly to the original claim

Write in an analytical, impersonal register appropriate to a high-quality policy journal or a senior strategic assessment brief.

## Task 15 — Diplomatic Letter: Proposed Amendments to a Disinformation Convention
*Level 4 · formal_correspondence · 250–400 words*

You are the Permanent Representative of your country to the Council of Europe. A proposed Convention on Cross-Border Online Disinformation (CCOD) would require signatory states to enact domestic legislation empowering a designated national body to mandate social media platforms to remove content classified as "systematically false and harmful" within 24 hours of notification. Your government's constitutional advisory panel and three civil society organisations have submitted formal opinions identifying freedom-of-expression and rule-of-law concerns in the mandatory removal provisions, while affirming support for the convention's overall objective.

Write a formal letter to the Secretary General of the Council of Europe in which you:

- Affirm your government's commitment to the Council's broader media integrity agenda and to the convention's stated objectives
- Set out, with precision and without redundancy, the constitutional and freedom-of-expression concerns raised by the mandatory removal provisions, with reference to the relevant ECHR article
- Propose two specific textual modifications that would bring the provisions into conformity with the relevant ECHR standard while preserving the convention's operational effectiveness
- Confirm your government's intention to participate constructively in the final round of negotiations

Observe the full conventions of formal multilateral diplomatic correspondence throughout.

## Task 16 — Essay: The Economics of Supply Chain Reshoring
*Level 4 · essay · 350–700 words*

Assess the following argument: "The strategic imperative to reshore critical supply chains for reasons of national security is, in the long run, economically self-defeating: it raises costs, reduces efficiency, and risks provoking the very trade fragmentation it purports to prevent."

Your essay must:

- State a clear, arguable thesis in the introduction that responds directly to the claim
- Present and evaluate the main economic objections to reshoring, drawing on appropriate analytical concepts such as comparative advantage, deadweight loss, and value chain efficiency
- Identify and interrogate the key assumptions or gaps in the "self-defeating" argument
- Distinguish between different types or scales of reshoring intervention and assess whether the critique applies with equal force to all of them
- Reach a substantiated conclusion that specifies the conditions under which reshoring is, or is not, strategically and economically justified

Write in an analytical, impersonal register consistent with quality economic journalism or a policy brief addressed to senior decision-makers.
