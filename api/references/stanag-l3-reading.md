# STANAG 6001 Reading — level descriptors (grounding)

The JFLT reading paper is **multilevel**: items climb from Level 1 to Level 4, 15 items per level.
Each item is a short self-contained text followed by one four-option multiple-choice question.
Generate items that match the target level's descriptor below. Level 3 is the C1 benchmark most
candidates aim for ("SLP 3").

Every descriptor has three facets:
- **Content** — the domains and text types the reader can handle.
- **Task** — what the reader can do (locate facts, infer, follow argument, detect stance/tone).
- **Accuracy** — how fully and reliably meaning is extracted.

## Level 1 — Survival
- Content: short, simple, predictable texts — signs, notices, labels, menus, timetables, very short
  personal messages. High-frequency vocabulary, present tense, concrete topics.
- Task: locate explicitly stated facts; match a word or phrase; understand a single simple instruction.
- Accuracy: understands the main concrete point when context is highly supportive.
- Question type: literal retrieval. Answer is stated almost verbatim.

## Level 2 — Functional
- Content: straightforward factual texts on familiar topics — routine correspondence, short news
  items, simple reports, instructions, descriptions. Mostly concrete, some simple connected prose.
- Task: understand main ideas and important supporting detail; follow a simple sequence of events or
  steps; grasp the writer's evident purpose.
- Accuracy: gets the gist and key facts; may miss nuance and lower-frequency vocabulary.
- Question type: main idea, specific detail, simple purpose. Answer requires reading a sentence or
  two, not just scanning a single word.

## Level 3 — Professional (C1 target)
- Content: long, dense, authentic, analytical prose on abstract and professional topics —
  geopolitics, security and defence policy, international affairs, economics, science and technology,
  society. Opinion pieces, editorials, policy analysis, in-depth reporting. Varied and sometimes
  low-frequency vocabulary, complex syntax, hypothetical and concessive structures, cohesive devices.
- Task: follow a sustained line of argument; infer meaning that is implied but not stated; identify
  the author's stance, attitude, and tone; distinguish fact from opinion; understand the function of
  a sentence within the argument (e.g. concession, example, qualification); resolve reference and
  cohesion across the text; infer the meaning of unfamiliar words from context.
- Accuracy: correctly interprets overall meaning and most detail under time pressure; may miss some
  regional or highly idiomatic forms.
- Question type: inference, author's opinion/attitude, tone, main argument, function of a paragraph,
  vocabulary-in-context, implication. The key must NOT be a verbatim lift from the text; it requires
  the reader to synthesise or infer. Distractors must be plausible to a weak reader and anchored in
  the text (a misread, an over-generalisation, a true-but-irrelevant statement, a reversed relation).

## Level 4 — Expert
- Content: highly abstract, sophisticated, and stylistically varied texts — professional literature,
  subtle commentary, irony, humour, implicit cultural reference, dense academic or literary prose.
- Task: grasp fine nuance, implication, irony, register shifts, and rhetorical strategy; appreciate
  stylistic effect; follow abstract reasoning in any field.
- Accuracy: near-complete comprehension including subtlety and connotation; recognises most
  regionalisms and allusions.
- Question type: nuance, irony, implied authorial judgement, rhetorical purpose, subtle contrast.
  Both the key and the distractors are defensible on a shallow reading; only full comprehension
  separates them.

## Passage length guidance (approximate)
- Level 1: 15–40 words.
- Level 2: 60–120 words.
- Level 3: 180–320 words.
- Level 4: 220–400 words.
